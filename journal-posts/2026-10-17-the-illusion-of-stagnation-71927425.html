The Illusion of Stagnation

Sometimes we mistake stillness for permanence. We look at our lives and see the same rooms, the same faces, the same routines, and then conclude that nothing has changed.

Yet nothing is ever quite as it was yesterday.

Change rarely ever announces itself. It happens in small, mostly imperceptible ways: in thoughts we no longer entertain, in feelings that have softened, and in the reprioritisation of what matters to us. Even things we believe to be fixed are always becoming something else.

Perhaps this is why we so often fail to recognise our own growth. We expect change to arrive as a grand event, something we can point to and say there, that was when everything became different.

But perhaps we are never truly standing still. Perhaps we are simply too close to the movement to notice it.

And there is something comforting in that thought, isn't there? That even when life appears unchanged, something within us is always shifting. We need not always know where we are going, nor recognise the moment we begin to become someone different.

Sometimes it is enough to trust that time is doing its work, even when we cannot see it.
